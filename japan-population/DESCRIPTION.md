In 2025, Japan recorded 1.59 million deaths and just 671,000 births. That's a natural decline of about 918,000 people in a single year, roughly a city the size of Austin, Texas. Deaths have outnumbered births every year since 2007.

Why is this happening, what does it mean for Japan, and why won't Japan be the last country to face it?

KEY NUMBERS
• Population: 83.2 million (1950) → peak of 128.1 million (2008) → 123.0 million (2025 census)
• 2025: 671,236 births, 1,589,489 deaths (Japanese nationals)
• Fertility rate: 1.14, a record low (replacement level is 2.1)
• Age 65 and over: 4.9% in 1950, 29.4% in 2025, about 39% projected for 2070
• About 9 million vacant homes (akiya), a record 13.8% of all housing
• Projection: about 87 million people by 2070 (IPSS, medium variant)

A NOTE ON THE DATA
Deaths first outnumbered births briefly in 2005; births edged ahead again in 2006, and deaths have exceeded births every year since 2007. Births have fallen overall since the mid-1970s, with small rises in a few years along the way. Deaths in 2025 dipped slightly from 2024 for the first time in five years, but remain near record highs.
