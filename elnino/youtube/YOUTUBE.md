📌 KEY FACTS
• WMO forecasts the Niño 3.4 region to average about +3.7 °C above normal in Oct–Dec 2026 (1993–2009 baseline), peaking around December. The previous record was +2.6 °C (Nov 2015–Jan 2016).
• WMO: "El Niño is firmly established and strengthening rapidly", with a near-100% chance it lasts through February 2027. It could be one of the strongest since at least 1950.
• NOAA declares El Niño when Niño 3.4 stays at least +0.5 °C above normal for five overlapping 3-month seasons.
• Past strong events (NOAA ONI peaks): 1982–83 +2.1 °C, 1997–98 +2.4 °C, 2015–16 +2.6 °C.
• Researchers estimate the 1982–83 and 1997–98 El Niños cost the global economy $4.1 and $5.7 trillion in the five years after each event (2017 US$).
• The Met Office says 2027 is very likely to replace 2024 as the warmest year on record.

Note: 2026 values are forecasts. NOAA's ONI and WMO use different baselines, as labelled on the chart. Maps of sea temperature and regional impacts are stylised illustrations based on the sources below.
